Download the signal.

Five minutes and 28 seconds. Ten seconds of midrange noise to set volume with, then five bursts of pink noise, one for each microphone position. Every burst is announced by a marker the app listens for.

Files.

test-signal-48k.wav Download 48 kHz, stereo, 16 bit. Start here. Both channels carry the same signal.
test-signal-44k.wav Download 44.1 kHz. For older head units that will not play 48 kHz, or that resample it badly.
test-signal-left.wav Download Left channel only, right channel digitally silent. For finding out which side is the problem.
test-signal-right.wav Download Right channel only. Measure one side, then the other, then compare.

The MP3 is a fallback. Use it only if the head unit refuses WAV.

Pink noise is the worst case input for a perceptual codec. The encoder's entire job is to discard content it believes is masked, and noise is nothing but such content. Plenty of encoders also lowpass somewhere between 16 and 19 kHz, and every user of such a file would measure a cliff at the top of the spectrum and blame their tweeters.

The file below was encoded at 320 kbps and then measured against the WAV it came from. It holds to within 0.1 dB from 20 Hz to 20 kHz and rolls off above 20.5 kHz, so this particular file is clean. What we cannot see is the decoder inside your head unit. Tell the app you used MP3 and it will lower its confidence in the top octave, because it has no way to check what actually came out of the speakers.

Putting it on a USB stick.

Most of the trouble people have here is the stick, not the file.

  • Format the stick as FAT32. Many head units cannot read exFAT or NTFS, and most of them say nothing useful when they fail. They just show an empty folder.
  • Keep the filename short. Some units still choke on long names. Renaming it to test.wav is fine. The app does not care what the file is called.
  • Put it in the root directory. Not inside a folder. Nested folders are the second most common reason a unit shows nothing.
  • One file at a time. If the stick has music on it too, the unit may shuffle or autoplay something else halfway through.

What is inside the file.

Everything sits at 20 dB below full scale. That is not a mastering choice. Many head units apply soft limiting or loudness normalisation to USB playback, and a hot signal triggers it. You would then be measuring the limiter rather than the speakers, and nothing in the result would tell you so.

Playing it some other way.

Any of these are fine, because none of them are the phone doing the measuring.

  • SD card in the head unit.
  • AUX cable from a laptop or a second phone.
  • CarPlay or Android Auto from a second phone.
  • Bluetooth from a second phone. This is fine. That phone has no microphone open.
  • The app's own generator mode on a second phone, which needs no file at all.
