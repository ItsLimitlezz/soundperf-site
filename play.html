The five positions.

The signal plays five bursts of noise. You measure one microphone position in each, and the app averages them.

This is not optional and it is not calibration. One measurement taken at one point in a car is mostly interference between the direct sound and the first reflections off the glass and the dashboard. Those notches move if the phone moves two centimetres, so they describe where you were holding it rather than what your system does. Five positions inside a head sized volume average that away and leave something real.

  1. Five positions. Start at the centre of the headrest at ear height, then move roughly 15 cm left, right, up, and forward. The app tells you which is which as you go.

Hold the phone with its bottom edge pointing at the windscreen, and keep your hand off the bottom of it. That is where the microphone this app uses actually is.

Set the level first.

The signal opens with ten seconds of midrange noise. That is there so you can set volume once and have it still be right when the measurement starts. Both parts sit at the same loudness.

  • Turn it up until it is clearly louder than the room, and no further. Loud conversation is plenty. The app draws a band to aim for and anywhere inside it measures the same.
  • Louder is not better. Once the signal is clearly above the room, more level buys nothing. The app averages 57 seconds of it, and that averaging has far more to give than the last few decibels of volume do.
  • Past that, it starts costing you. Pink noise peaks about 14 dB above its own average, so a level that sounds merely loud can be clipping at the peaks. The app throws those recordings away rather than analysing them, because clipped bass looks exactly like a speaker running out of travel.
  • This is five minutes of continuous broadband noise and you are sitting in it. That is harmless at a sensible level and unpleasant well before it is dangerous, but there is no reason to be anywhere near a level you would not want to sit next to.
  • Once it is set, leave it alone. Every position has to be measured at the same volume.

Before you press play.

  • Turn off loudness, bass boost, and any DSP preset on the head unit. Set every EQ band to zero.
  • Engine off. Doors shut. Windows up. Fan off.
  • The app does the rest. It listens for the marker tones and starts recording on its own.

Play this on one device. Measure on another.

Opening a microphone on a phone that is also sending Bluetooth audio drops the link to the narrowband call profile. The measurement then runs through an 8 kHz pipe and reports no error at all. It just shows a cliff at the top of the spectrum that looks like a tweeter problem. Keep the two jobs on two devices.
