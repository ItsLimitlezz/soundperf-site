What this is Play Download

Measure what your system actually does.

This page serves the test signal. You play it through the system you want to tune, and the app listens at the seat where you actually sit. It compares what it hears to a target and tells you what to set each band on your head unit to. That is the whole product.

Play it here.

Straight from the browser, through an AUX cable or Bluetooth from a laptop. Shows you which position is running so you know when to move the phone.

Or take it with you.

WAV files for a USB stick or an SD card. This is the path most head units like best.

How the measurement works.

  1. The signal plays from something that is not your phone. A USB stick, an SD card, a laptop on AUX, or a second phone. The measuring phone has to keep its microphone free.
  2. The app hears the marker and starts on its own. Three short tones before each burst of noise. No pairing, no countdown, no shouting across the car.
  3. You move through five positions. One measurement at one point is mostly interference patterns. Five around head height average out to something that describes the system rather than the exact spot you were holding the phone.
  4. The app gives you numbers to type in. One value per band. It also tells you the best result your hardware can reach, so you know when to stop turning knobs.

What this page will not do.

It will not measure anything. A browser cannot turn off the automatic gain control on a phone microphone, and that processing flattens level differences over time, which is exactly the thing being measured. A web page would produce a smooth, believable, mostly fictional curve with no error to catch it. So this page plays the signal and stops there.

Get the app.

Native iOS, because the measurement needs an audio session a browser cannot ask for. The app carries its own copy of this signal, so it works with no connection to this site.
